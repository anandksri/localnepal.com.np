01 / Local search

Make your business easier to find locally.

When someone searches for a service nearby, accurate information and a useful website can help them understand whether your business is the right fit.

Start with the essentials

  1. Check your business details. Use the same business name, phone number and relevant location information on your website and public profiles. Keep hours and contact details current.
  2. Review your Google Business Profile eligibility. If your business qualifies, claim or create its profile, select an accurate category and follow Google's guidelines for displaying your address or service area.
  3. Make each page useful. Explain what you offer, who it is for and where you serve customers. Use descriptive page titles and answer questions people actually ask.
  4. Earn genuine reviews. Invite feedback from real customers and respond thoughtfully. Never buy reviews or offer rewards in exchange for them.
  5. Check the experience on mobile. Make contact details easy to find, keep pages readable and test important links on a phone.

Start here: Search for your business name and check whether customers see accurate, consistent information. Search visibility can take time and is never guaranteed.

02 / Paid media

Before you spend
on advertising.

A useful campaign begins with a clear business objective, not simply a boosted post or a larger budget.

Plan the campaign

  1. Choose one primary goal. Decide whether you need qualified inquiries, sales, bookings or awareness. Define what a meaningful result looks like before choosing a platform.
  2. Understand your audience. Consider where customers are, what they need and what might stop them from choosing you. Write an offer that addresses those needs.
  3. Set a budget you can test. Choose an amount and time period your business can afford, including creative and management costs as well as ad spend. Avoid treating a test budget as a promise of results.
  4. Prepare the destination. Send people to a relevant page with a clear offer, useful details and a working way to contact or buy—not just a generic homepage.
  5. Measure and learn. Track relevant actions where possible, review the quality of inquiries as well as their cost, and change one meaningful variable at a time.

Start here: Write down your goal, audience, offer and budget on one page. Make sure your tracking and consent practices fit the platforms you use and applicable requirements.

03 / Website experience

Make the next step obvious.

Getting a visitor to your website is only the beginning. Help them quickly understand your offer and how to act.

Review the customer journey

  1. Lead with clarity. State what you do, who you help and why it matters in language a new visitor can understand.
  2. Give each page a purpose. Make the next action clear, whether it is calling, requesting information or buying. Use specific labels rather than vague buttons.
  3. Answer common questions. Include the details customers need to make a decision, such as service areas, process, relevant policies and how to reach you.
  4. Build trust honestly. Use accurate business details and real examples or testimonials only when you have permission to share them.
  5. Test the whole journey. On a phone and a computer, follow your own links and contact options. Remove unnecessary steps and check that every action works.

Start here: Ask someone unfamiliar with your business to find your main service and contact you. Notice where they hesitate and improve that step first.
